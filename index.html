Engineering Mathematics–I (BAS103)

AKTU Examination-Oriented Detailed Teacher Notes - Unit 1: Matrices

1. Introduction to Matrices & System Representation

Matrices numerical information ko compact form me systematic tarike se represent karne ke liye use hoti hain.

Applications: Computer Science, AI, Data Science, Machine Learning, Electrical/Mechanical Engineering, Dynamics.

System of Equations Representation:

$$\begin{cases} x + 2y = 5 \\ 3x + 4y = 11 \end{cases} \implies \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 11 \end{bmatrix}$$

2. Basic Definition, Order & Elements

Definition: Numbers, quantities, ya expressions ka rectangular arrangement rows aur columns me matrix kahlata hai. Capital letters $(A, B, C)$ se represent kiya jata hai.

General Form: $A = [a_{ij}]_{m \times n}$ jahan $m$ = rows, $n$ = columns, aur $a_{ij}$ element hai $i$-th row aur $j$-th column ka.

Order: Rows $\times$ Columns (Hamesha isi format me likhein, e.g., $2 \times 3$ ya $3 \times 2$).

3. Types of Matrices

Matrix Type Definition / Property Example Structure
Row Matrix Sirf 1 row hoti hai. Order: $1 \times n$ $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 & 7 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$
Column Matrix Sirf 1 column hota hai. Order: $m \times 1$ $\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$
Square Matrix Rows aur Columns equal hote hain ($m = n$) $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$
Zero / Null Matrix Sare elements zero hote hain ($O$) $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
Diagonal Matrix Principal diagonal ke alawa baki sare 0 hote hain. $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$
Scalar Matrix Diagonal matrix jiske diagonal elements equal hon ($A = kI$) $\begin{bmatrix} 5 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$
Identity Matrix ($I$) Diagonal elements 1 aur baki 0. Property: $AI = IA = A$ $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
Upper Triangular Principal diagonal ke niche ke sare elements zero hon. $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 0 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$
Lower Triangular Principal diagonal ke upar ke sare elements zero hon. $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 4 & 3 & 0 \\ 6 & 5 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$
Symmetric Matrix Transpose lene par unchanged rahe: $A^T = A$ ($a_{ij} = a_{ji}$) $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 3 & 4 & 7 \\ 5 & 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$
Skew-Symmetric $A^T = -A$. Diagonal elements hamesha 0 hote hain. $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 & -3 \\ -2 & 0 & 5 \\ 3 & -5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$

4. Transpose & Operations

Transpose ($A^T$): Rows ko columns me aur columns ko rows me interchange karna.

  • Properties: $(A^T)^T = A$, $(A+B)^T = A^T + B^T$, $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$ (Reversal Law)
  • Equality: Do matrices tabhi equal hoti hain jab order same ho aur corresponding elements identical hon.
  • Scalar Multiplication: Scalar $k$ matrix ke har element se multiply hota hai.

5. Matrix Multiplication (Solved Example)

Order condition: Agar $A$ ka order $m \times n$ aur $B$ ka order $n \times p$ hai, tabhi $AB$ exist karega (Order: $m \times p$).

Properties: Not commutative ($AB \neq BA$), Associative [$A(BC) = (AB)C$], Distributive [$A(B+C) = AB + AC$].

Solved Example: Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$. Find $AB$.

$$AB = \begin{bmatrix} (1\times 5 + 2\times 7) & (1\times 6 + 2\times 8) \\ (3\times 5 + 4\times 7) & (3\times 6 + 4\times 8) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 19 & 22 \\ 43 & 50 \end{bmatrix}$$

6. Determinants, Minors & Inverse

Adjoint ($\text{adj } A$): Cofactor matrix ka transpose $[C]^T$.

Inverse Formula: $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \text{adj}(A)$, jahan $|A| \neq 0$.

Singular vs Non-Singular: Agar $|A| = 0$, toh matrix singular hai (Inverse exist nahi karta). Agar $|A| \neq 0$, toh Non-Singular hai.

7. Rank of Matrix & System Consistency

Rank ($\rho(A)$): Matrix me maximum number of linearly independent rows. Row Echelon form me non-zero rows hi Rank hoti hai.

System Consistency ($AX = B$):

  • Unique Solution: $\rho(A) = \rho([A|B]) = n$ (variables count)
  • Infinitely Many Solutions: $\rho(A) = \rho([A|B]) < n$
  • No Solution: $\rho(A) \neq \rho([A|B])$

8. Cayley-Hamilton Theorem & Eigenvalues

Characteristic Equation: $|A - \lambda I| = 0$. Iske roots hi Eigenvalues hote hain.

Cayley-Hamilton Theorem: Har square matrix apni characteristic equation ko satisfy karti hai. Agar $\lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 = 0$ hai, toh $A^2 - 4A + 3I = 0$ hoga.

Applications: Higher powers of matrices ($A^4, A^8$) aur Inverse ($A^{-1}$) nikalne me.
